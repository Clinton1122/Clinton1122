Good to know before you go

Dress for temples

Cover shoulders and knees, and take off shoes and hats before you go in. A light sarong does the job.

Start early

Sites open around 7 AM. You beat the heat and the coach groups, and the light is better for photos.

Best months

January to April for the Cultural Triangle and Kandy. The south coast forts are best from December to March.

What should I wear to temples in Sri Lanka?

Cover your shoulders and knees, and take off your shoes and hat before you enter. A light scarf or sarong is enough. Don’t pose with your back to a Buddha statue for photos.

Are entry tickets included?

Each listing says what’s included. Many cultural tours include entry to the main site. Look for “Entry included” on the listing.

When is the best time for the Cultural Triangle?

January to April is driest. Start early to avoid the midday heat. Poya (full moon) days are busy at temples but often very special to see.

Can I cancel?

Most tours have free cancellation up to 48 hours before the start time. Some hosts use their own policy, which is shown on the listing before you book.

Sri Lanka’s culture and heritage, explained

The Cultural Triangle

Between Anuradhapura, Polonnaruwa and Kandy lies the Cultural Triangle, home to five of Sri Lanka’s eight UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Anuradhapura was the island’s first capital for over a thousand years, and its giant stupas are still places of worship today. Polonnaruwa followed, with its stone Buddhas at Gal Vihara. Sigiriya, the rock fortress, rises between them.

Kandy and the Temple of the Tooth

Kandy was the last royal capital before the British arrived in 1815. The Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic holds Sri Lanka’s most important Buddhist relic, and the evening and morning offerings are open to visitors. In July or August, the Esala Perahera fills the streets with dancers, drummers and elephants.

Colonial forts and towns

Portuguese, Dutch and British traders each left their mark. Galle Fort is the best preserved, with ramparts, churches and merchant houses you can walk in an afternoon. Jaffna in the north has a different story again, shaped by Tamil Hindu culture and its own fort.

Choosing a guide

A good guide makes the difference between seeing a site and understanding it. Look for the SLTDA registered badge: these guides have passed Sri Lanka Tourism’s training and exam. Private tours let you set the pace, and small-group tours are often cheaper.
