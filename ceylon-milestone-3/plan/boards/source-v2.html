Dashboard plan · for Akila · 25 Nov 2026

A refund that's due in 7 hours shouldn't look like one that closed last week

Today's Refunds page and the canvas both give every request the same weight: a row in a table. Partners mostly work on their phones, between trips. They should be able to see who's waiting for them, how long they have and what it costs, without reading a table. These five decisions shape every screen in the plan.

Today, app2.ceylonnooks.com. Four summary cards, a banner with the old 24 hour rule, then a table. LKR on the cards, $ in the rows. On a phone the table is 970 px wide.

The canvas fixes the rules and the wording, and it's the content we keep. Its layout is the same pattern, though: four equal cards, filter tabs, a five-column table and a drawer.

  1. 01One product, not three

    Partner and admin use the traveller site's header, tab tray, page width and photos. A partner who has browsed Ceylon Nooks already knows how to get around.

  2. 02What needs you comes first, at full size

    A request that needs a reply is a large card with the traveller's own words, the deadline and the replies, ready to act on. Everything waiting on someone else is a quiet list below it. There are no summary cards.

  3. 03Time is the headline

    Deadlines use the same big clock as the traveller's pickup time, in Sri Lanka time and with the real date. Policies are drawn as a line of real dates, with a marker where the traveller asked.

  4. 04Money reads like a receipt

    Paid, refund, fee, what the traveller gets and what changes in the payout, one line each with the total underneath. The same receipt appears on partner, admin and statement screens.

  5. 05Colour only for “act now”

    Red is for things with a deadline on you. Everything else is plain words with a small icon. With less colour on screen, the urgent items are easy to spot.

What we keep

Every rule, status and line of copy from the brief and the canvas. The style guide's colours (the same as the traveller site), Bricolage Grotesque and Manrope, the spacing scale, borders on cards instead of shadows, and the 12 and 18 px corners.

Partner and admin are built differently on purpose

Partners have a few requests a week, mostly on a phone, so their pages are calm and use the traveller site's 1136 px width. The admin team works through a queue on a laptop, so admin pages use the full width: the list on the left and the request on the right, with no drawer opening and closing.

Partner · Refunds

Who's waiting, how long you have, what it costs

Built for a partner opening this between trips: the answer to “do I need to do anything?” is the first sentence on the page.

  1. 1The headline sentence does the summary“2 travellers are waiting. Daniel's is due first, in 7 h 55 min.” It replaces the four summary cards, and it reads the same on a phone.
  2. 2A request is a ticket, not a rowThe photo, the traveller's own words and their evidence on the left. The replies sit under their words. On the right, the stub with the clock, the same shape as the traveller's pickup card.
  3. 3The policy is drawn as real datesGreen, amber and grey stretches with the date each one ends, and a marker where the traveller asked. The partner can see at a glance why the request came to them.
  4. 4Offering another date is the main buttonThe booking stays, nobody pays a fee, and it's the outcome the brief prefers. The other three replies sit next to it in the same row, so none is hidden.
  5. 5Everything else is quieterRequests waiting on the traveller or on our team are plain rows with a sentence each. Closed ones fold into a single line. Money for the month is one small receipt.

From the canvas: the statuses, the countdown rules, the four replies, the payout preview and every amount. Changed: the layout and the order of things on the page.

Admin · Refunds

A queue the team can get through

Decisions are what admins do here all day. The list and the request sit side by side, so moving to the next one is a single click.

  1. 1Ordered by when the trip startsThe trip is the real deadline, so it's on the right of every item: as hours and in red inside 48 hours, as a date after that.
  2. 2The partner's stance is in words“Partner agreed to 50% back” instead of a pill. You can triage the queue without opening anything.
  3. 3Three facts, side by sideWhat the policy gives, what the partner said and the traveller's history. The “policy changed since booking” warning sits right under the policy it's about.
  4. 4Choices on the left, the receipt on the rightEvery change to the refund or the fee updates the receipt, down to what comes out of the partner's payout and our commission.
  5. 5Bulk only where it's safeThe green strip appears only when partners agreed, no trip has started and nothing is flagged, the same rules as the canvas.

From the canvas: the eight reasons, the fee options, the warnings, guest history and bulk rules. Changed: a split view instead of a table and a drawer, and the photo header so each request is easy to recognise.

Partner · phone

Made for a phone in one hand, between trips

It'll be installed as an app for about a year, so the phone layout is designed first, not squeezed down from desktop.

  1. 01Tabs at the bottom

    Today, Bookings, Refunds and Messages are a thumb away. Everything else is under More.

  2. 02Reply right from the card

    The four replies are on the request itself. One tap opens the reply with that choice already picked.

  3. 03Cancel a whole day

    Weather usually affects everyone booked that day. Tap the day, then cancel it once, with evidence and new bookings stopped. Agreed by Akila on 23 Nov.

Red means it's on you

The same status is red for one person and calm for the others. A reply needed is red for the partner and a plain line for the traveller. A request to decide is red for admin and amber for the partner. Travellers get orange text only when they have something to accept.

Words carry the facts

Amounts and real dates go in the status whenever they fit. Times are Sri Lanka time, and each screen says so once. “Our team aims to decide by…” uses Akila's 2 to 3 days and never promises. The fee is always called a processing fee.
