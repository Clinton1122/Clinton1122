Off-brand

Orange (#FF6B35) everywhere

Orange is not in the logo, packaging or uniforms. It's in links, prices, labels and the browser theme colour. The yellow on the site (#FFD23F) also differs from the logo yellow (#FEDC22).

Off-brand

Template layout

Rounded white cards, coloured icon tiles (“How it works”, “Who we serve”), centred headings, a scrolling marquee and an emoji in the menu (“💰 Earn”). Nothing says Lumey.

Trust

Claims and numbers that conflict

“Nigeria's No 1”, “save up to 85%”, “Over 518 sold”. Three phone numbers in the header, an Akure address that differs from the flyer, a Gmail address, and an empty blog section.

Data

Model names and prices don't match the pricing sheet

The site and the box say PowerBox 550; the PRD says 600. The site says 3kVA, 6kVA and 12kVA where the sheet says 2.5, 5 and 10. The 1500 bundle is ₦530,000 on the site and ₦500,000 in the PRD.

Photos

Most product photos on the site look computer-generated

The wooden-table shots of the 550, 1500 and 1900 show a US-style socket and audio connectors that the real units don't have. The 3100, 4000 and 8000 shots (boxes on wheels in offices) and a file named “Realistic Solar Generator Scene” look AI-made too. Your real studio photos on the clay backdrop are better and more honest; the new homepage uses those. The heavy-duty range still needs a real shoot.

3. The four references, studied properly

What each does well, and what we take from it.

LetsGoSolar, product page

  • Price in the hero, big, beside one clear headline (“NEPA went off. Life ON.”).
  • Order form right on the page: state, LGA, name, WhatsApp number. Ordering is two steps.
  • Runtime calculator with appliance buttons and a big result.
  • “Three months of fuel could buy your YC1100”: the price crossed out against a year of petrol.
  • Lifestyle scenes (work, family, shop), each with the appliances it runs.
  • Specs in an accordion beside a large product view, Instagram video testimonials, FAQ.

We take: the price-first hero, the appliance picker, the fuel comparison and the lifestyle scenes. We skip: the navy and the cartoon sunburst; your colours are yellow, black and clay.

Reeddi, homepage

  • One confident headline, lots of white space, a single accent colour.
  • A full-width documentary photo of people building the product.
  • Press quotes and partner logos straight after the hero.
  • One big impact number, video testimonials, and a clean footer with both offices.

We take: the confidence, the full-width “people who build it” photo (your team shots), and proof straight after the products. We skip: numbers we can't back up, and their generic green newsletter band.

Thrillhouse, buying and instalments

  • “Pay small small” said plainly, on the homepage and on every package.
  • A “What can it power?” table: appliance, quantity, maximum power, recommended hours.
  • Add to cart and a Klump instalment option at checkout, plus a free-delivery bar.

We take: “pay small small” as a headline promise, and the appliance table for each product page. We skip: the cluttered shop layout and stock banners.

EcoFlow Nigeria

  • Photography does the selling: real homes, the product in use.
  • Each product card shows capacity, output and price, with the old price crossed out.
  • Ranges grouped as series, a “why buy here” block, and customer reviews with photos.

We take: photo-first sections and capacity-plus-price on every card. We skip: the endless rails and pop-ups.
